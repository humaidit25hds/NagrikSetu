# NagrikSetu Competitor Analysis

## Comparison Snapshot

| Alternative | Strength | Friction for citizens | Opportunity for NagrikSetu |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UMANG | Broad access to public services in one app | Large catalogue can feel difficult to scan; service language can remain formal | Add guided discovery, plain-language summaries, and a focused next step |
| DigiLocker | Trusted document storage and sharing | Users may not know which document is needed or how it relates to a service | Explain document purpose and link document preparation to the service journey |
| MyScheme | Useful scheme discovery and eligibility information | Users still have to interpret criteria and complete the next research step themselves | Present potentially relevant schemes with guided questions and official verification |
| State government portals | Authoritative local information | Different layouts, terminology, and processes across states | Create a consistent orientation layer while sending users to the official source |
| Cyber cafes / assisted service centres | Human help for people with low digital confidence | Travel, queues, cost, privacy, and inconsistent quality | Offer a safe first step and make escalation to human help explicit |
| Generic AI assistants | Natural-language conversation | May hallucinate, lack official context, or give unsafe advice | Ground answers in official links, show uncertainty, and protect sensitive data |

## Market Gaps

1. Most public-service tools expose a catalogue; fewer help a citizen decide where to begin.
2. Search often assumes the user already knows the formal name of a service.
3. Eligibility language can sound more certain than the available evidence allows.
4. Safety guidance is rarely placed directly inside the moment of action.
5. Accessibility and senior-friendly interaction are often treated as settings rather than core flows.

## NagrikSetu Positioning

NagrikSetu should act as a trusted bridge between a citizen's everyday question and the official service process. It should not replace government portals, make eligibility decisions, collect unnecessary personal data, or submit an application without the user's informed action.

## Differentiating Features

- Setu AI orb available across the experience
- Voice-first and typed conversation
- Category browsing for users who do not know the official service name
- Plain-language service summaries
- Document checklist and step-by-step preparation
- “Potentially relevant” scheme suggestions instead of eligibility claims
- Official portal links and verification cues
- Inline fraud and privacy reminders
- Senior Citizen Mode with larger type and reduced complexity

## Competitive Design Requirements

- Keep the visual system calm and high-contrast despite the futuristic orb.
- Make the official source link as visible as the assistant's recommendation.
- Use cards for scanning, but avoid hiding critical warnings behind interaction.
- Ensure every AI answer has a clear path to correction, source checking, or human assistance.
